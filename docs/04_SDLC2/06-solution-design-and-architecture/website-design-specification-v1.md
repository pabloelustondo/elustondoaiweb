## 1. Design intent

The Elustondo.ai website must communicate technical seriousness, practical experience, conceptual integrity, and disciplined experimentation. It should feel like a mature engineering practice with an editorial voice—not a generic AI startup, an aggressive agency landing page, or a personal résumé.

The visual system should support careful reading, credible technical content, selected project evidence, and a growing body of articles. It must remain restrained enough that the work, writing, and ideas are the primary content.

Core design qualities:
- quiet and serious;
- editorial rather than promotional;
- modern but not trend-driven;
- technically credible;
- family-led and collaborative without becoming sentimental;
- simple enough to evolve without repeated redesign.

## 2. Brand identity

The website must use the official Elustondo.ai brand assets maintained under C7-Corporation / Brand & Marketing. The logo is not to be redrawn, approximated, recoloured, stretched, simplified, or regenerated by an AI tool.

Approved website uses:
- Header: Elustondo.ai-Logo-Primary.svg preferred; PNG fallback where SVG is unsuitable.
- Formal corporate identification: Elustondo.ai-Logo-Corporate.svg or PNG.
- Favicon and compact browser/app icon: Elustondo.ai-Icon-Square.svg and Elustondo.ai-Icon-1024.png.
- Shield-only usage: Elustondo.ai-Shield.svg, only where a compact mark is required.
- Article signature: Elustondo.ai-Article-Signature.png.
- Email signature: Elustondo.ai-Email-Signature-Mark.png.

The website project may consume these files but must not create competing logo variants.

## 3. Visual language

### 3.1 Colour

Primary visual identity:
- deep Elustondo blue for headings, navigation, links, and principal controls;
- official shield blue and gold only through approved assets or documented design tokens;
- warm white or very light neutral background;
- dark neutral body text;
- restrained border and muted text colours.

Avoid:
- bright startup gradients;
- neon AI palettes;
- excessive blue backgrounds;
- decorative glows;
- black-heavy luxury styling;
- arbitrary colour variation between pages.

Final production colour values must be sampled from the official vector assets and recorded as design tokens in the codebase.

### 3.2 Typography

The design uses two complementary typographic roles:
- Serif display type for major page titles, article titles, and selected editorial headings.
- Clean sans-serif type for navigation, body copy, metadata, buttons, labels, and technical information.

Typography should be readable, spacious, and modest. Headings should not be oversized merely for visual drama. Body text should preserve comfortable line length and line spacing.

The implementation should prefer reliable system or openly licensed web fonts with good performance. Font loading must not block meaningful content.

### 3.3 Layout

Desktop layout:
- centred content container with generous but controlled margins;
- slim, calm header;
- primary content area with optional right rail on the homepage;
- modular sections separated by light rules or whitespace;
- card use limited to discrete articles, projects, or external links;
- no dashboard appearance.

Mobile layout:
- single-column content;
- compact but accessible header and navigation;
- no horizontal overflow;
- article/project cards stack vertically;
- sidebar content moves into the main reading sequence;
- tap targets remain large enough for touch use.

### 3.4 AI-assisted interface layout

The stable shell must accommodate two related areas:

- an Assistant Panel for questions, navigation requests, suggestions, status, and refinement;
- a large Generated Page Canvas containing the isolated iframe when a tailored page is returned.

On large screens the Assistant Panel may appear as a restrained right rail or another clearly separated control area. The generated page remains the dominant reading surface. Exact placement should be resolved through responsive mockups and testing rather than fixed prematurely.

On narrow screens the panel and canvas enter one logical reading order. The design must not shrink the iframe beside a narrow persistent chat column. Permanent navigation, direct page access, downloads, and contact remain usable when the Assistant Panel is unavailable.

The assistant may feel conversational, but long answers appear as formatted pages in the canvas rather than accumulating only as chat bubbles.

## 4. Homepage structure

### 4.1 Header

The header uses the official primary logo on the left. Navigation should remain concise and stable. The current information-architecture candidates are:

- Home
- Services
- Articles
- Projects or Experience
- Resources
- Methods
- People
- Ask Elustondo.ai
- About
- Contact

Final labels and grouping require responsive information-architecture review; the product responsibilities must remain reachable even if some destinations move into secondary navigation.

External destinations such as LinkedIn, Medium, and GitHub may appear as simple text or icon links on large screens, and should move into a menu or footer on small screens.

### 4.2 Hero

The hero is editorial, not cinematic. It should include:
- a small corporate identifier: Elustondo Systems Consulting Inc.;
- the principal statement: “Spec-driven AI-assisted software engineering, solution architecture, agentic systems, and modernization.”;
- a short explanation of the practice;
- two restrained actions, initially “Read our work” and “View projects.”

The hero may use a subtle abstract technical pattern, but must not depend on generic stock AI imagery, robots, glowing brains, code rain, or fabricated office scenes.

### 4.3 Selected writing

Display three curated articles with:
- a controlled visual thumbnail or abstract cover;
- article title;
- concise summary;
- destination indicator if the article opens on Medium or another publication.

Initial candidate themes:
- Spec-Driven AI-Assisted Software Engineering;
- Responsibility-Driven Design for the Agentic AI Era;
- Simple Context Architecture for Personal Multi-Agent Systems.

Article content and titles must come from the canonical article system, not from placeholder copy once production work begins.

### 4.4 Current work

Feature one or two active projects. VISTA may be shown as the first project, using only claims that have been reviewed and approved for public disclosure.

The project presentation should include:
- project name;
- current status;
- one-sentence problem statement;
- short evidence-based description;
- link to a project page or approved case study.

Do not publish confidential client data, unverified performance claims, private commercial arrangements, or client logos without authorization.

### 4.5 People

The initial people section may include Pablo Elustondo, Santiago Elustondo, Rodrigo Elustondo, and Williams Martinez, subject to final role and consent review.

Use real approved photographs or a consistent non-photographic fallback. Do not use generated portraits or approximate likenesses.

Roles must be accurate and current. Avoid inflated titles. The section should communicate a family-led collaborative practice while preserving professional clarity.

### 4.6 Right rail / secondary content

On large screens, a restrained right rail may contain:
- Follow on Medium;
- Connect on LinkedIn;
- GitHub;
- a small “work in progress” note.

Suggested editorial statement:
“This site will evolve gradually as projects mature and articles are revised and curated. Clarity over volume. Substance over noise.”

On narrow screens this content joins the main flow after the principal homepage sections.

### 4.7 Footer

The footer should include:
- © 2026 Elustondo Systems Consulting Inc.;
- Toronto, Canada;
- pablo@elustondo.ai or the final approved contact address;
- Privacy;
- Terms, only when a real terms page exists.

Do not publish the registered residential office address, personal phone number, or incorporation-document details on the public website unless there is a specific legal or business requirement.

## 5. Secondary pages

### 5.1 Articles
A curated index of published writing. Medium may remain the publishing source for v1, with Elustondo.ai providing summaries, themes, and links. Avoid duplicating full articles until a publishing strategy is approved.

### 5.2 Projects
Selected public work and experiments. Each project page should distinguish:
- problem and context;
- intended users;
- design and architecture;
- evidence and current status;
- collaborators;
- limitations and next steps.

### 5.3 Methods
Explain the practice’s engineering methods without turning the page into marketing slogans. Initial themes may include SDLC², responsibility-driven design, SOLID 2.0, specification-driven work, evidence, observability, and disciplined AI assistance.

### 5.4 People
Short biographies, current roles, areas of contribution, and approved external links.

Approved LinkedIn destinations and downloadable résumé or professional-profile PDFs may be provided when the person, content, publication scope, and file version have been reviewed. A public résumé is a controlled publication artifact and must not be generated from unverified profile text.

### 5.5 About
Explain Elustondo.ai as the public brand of Elustondo Systems Consulting Inc., a Canadian technology practice. Present history and values selectively, with emphasis on useful experience rather than nostalgia.

### 5.6 Contact
A simple direct contact path. For v1, an email link may be preferable to a complex backend form. A form should be added only when spam handling, delivery, privacy, and operational ownership are defined.

## 6. Content principles

- Evidence before claims.
- Clarity before volume.
- Practical work before abstract positioning.
- No invented client testimonials.
- No invented team biographies.
- No filler metrics.
- No “revolutionary”, “cutting-edge”, “world-class”, or similar unsupported language.
- Distinguish active projects, experiments, articles, methods, and completed work.
- Keep first-person language deliberate: use “we” for the practice and “I” only in explicitly personal writing.

## 8. Responsive behaviour

Target widths should be content-driven rather than device-brand-driven. At minimum:
- desktop: multi-column homepage where appropriate;
- tablet: reduced spacing and simplified columns;
- mobile: single-column flow and compact navigation.

Acceptance criteria:
- no clipped text;
- no horizontal scrolling at 320 CSS pixels;
- readable navigation without overlapping the logo;
- images preserve aspect ratio;
- cards remain legible without fixed heights;
- sidebar content appears in a logical mobile order.

## 9. Accessibility

The site should target WCAG 2.2 AA practices appropriate to a small corporate site:
- semantic HTML;
- keyboard-operable navigation;
- visible focus states;
- sufficient colour contrast;
- alt text for meaningful images;
- empty alt text for decorative images;
- headings in logical order;
- reduced-motion respect;
- no information communicated only through colour;
- form labels and accessible validation if a form is added.

## 10. Image and graphical resource rules

Every website-specific image must have:
- a clear filename;
- source or creator;
- creation date;
- intended page/section;
- copyright or permission status;
- approved alt text;
- original source file where available;
- optimized web export.

Canonical logos remain in Brand & Marketing. Website-specific visuals belong under Elustondo.ai Website / 05 Website-Specific Assets.

Recommended filename pattern:
Elustondo.ai-[Page]-[Purpose]-[Variant]-[Width].[ext]

Examples:
Elustondo.ai-Home-Hero-Pattern-Desktop-1600.webp
Elustondo.ai-Articles-SDLC2-Card-800.webp
Elustondo.ai-OpenGraph-Default-1200x630.png

## 11. Motion and interaction

Interaction should be subtle:
- small hover and focus changes;
- no autoplay video;
- no heavy parallax;
- no decorative cursor effects;
- no animation that delays reading;
- motion disabled or reduced for users who prefer reduced motion.

## 12. Design exclusions

Do not introduce:
- generic AI-generated people;
- fake product screenshots;
- decorative dashboards;
- excessive iconography;
- animated backgrounds;
- complex CMS controls in v1;
- customer login, member administration, or payment flows in the initial public release; future authenticated applications require separate approved requirements and security design;
- unreviewed marketing copy;
- duplicate or modified logos.

## 13. Design QA gate

The design is ready for implementation when:
- the official primary logo is correctly placed and scaled;
- production page scope is confirmed;
- homepage copy is reviewed;
- team roles and photographs are approved;
- article and project selections are approved;
- mobile and desktop layouts are represented;
- accessibility constraints are documented;
- all placeholder or invented content is clearly marked or removed.

## 14. Current decision

The July 2026 editorial rendering is the visual baseline, not a finished production page. The implementation must preserve its calm structure and information hierarchy while replacing every approximate logo, invented portrait, uncertain role, sample link, and placeholder claim with canonical assets and reviewed content.
